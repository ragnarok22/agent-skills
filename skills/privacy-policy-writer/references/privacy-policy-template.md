# Privacy Policy Template

Use this as a drafting structure for generated privacy policies. Adapt sections to the product, jurisdiction, data practices, and user instructions. Do not keep placeholder text in the final document unless the fact is genuinely missing.

## Default Document Structure

# Privacy Policy

**Effective Date:** [Effective Date]

[Company Legal Name] ("[Company Short Name]", "we", "us", or "our") operates [Product Name]. This Privacy Policy explains how we collect, use, disclose, retain, and protect personal information when users access or use [Product Name].

## 1. Scope

Explain what product, website, app, API, or service the policy covers.

Include only if relevant:

- Website visitors.
- Registered users.
- Organization/team users.
- API users.
- Mobile app users.
- Trial, paid, or enterprise customers.

## 2. Information We Collect

Group data by source.

### Information Users Provide

Use when relevant:

- Account details such as name, email address, username, password, organization, role, and profile details.
- Contact and support messages.
- Billing details, plan selections, tax information, and payment-related records.
- User content, uploaded files, comments, messages, forms, prompts, generated output, projects, preferences, and settings.
- Marketing preferences, survey responses, feedback, and communication choices.

### Information Collected Automatically

Use when relevant:

- Usage data, log data, device data, browser data, IP address, pages viewed, referring URLs, timestamps, feature interactions, diagnostic data, and crash logs.
- Cookies, local storage, pixels, SDK identifiers, advertising identifiers, or similar technologies.
- Approximate location inferred from IP address.

### Information From Third Parties

Use when relevant:

- Identity providers and single sign-on providers.
- Payment processors.
- Integration partners.
- App stores.
- Marketing, referral, attribution, or analytics providers.

Avoid claiming third-party collection unless code, packages, or user-provided facts support it.

## 3. How We Use Information

Map purposes to collected data categories where possible.

Common purposes:

- Provide, operate, secure, and maintain the service.
- Create and manage accounts.
- Authenticate users and prevent fraud or abuse.
- Process payments, subscriptions, invoices, taxes, refunds, and billing support.
- Store, process, display, transmit, or generate user content.
- Provide customer support and respond to requests.
- Analyze usage, debug issues, improve features, and measure performance.
- Send service messages, security notices, product updates, and marketing communications where permitted.
- Personalize the user experience.
- Comply with legal obligations and enforce terms.

## 4. AI Features And User Content

Use only when the product includes AI, automation, model inference, content generation, transcription, embeddings, agents, or similar features.

Cover:

- Types of inputs sent to AI services, such as prompts, files, messages, metadata, or context.
- Whether third-party AI providers process the data.
- Whether user content may be used to provide, secure, improve, or debug AI features.
- Any explicit user-provided rule about model training. Do not invent a no-training promise.

## 5. Cookies And Similar Technologies

Use when the product has a website, analytics, ads, consent tooling, session cookies, local storage, tracking pixels, or SDK identifiers.

Cover:

- Essential cookies or local storage for login, security, preferences, and session management.
- Analytics cookies or SDKs.
- Marketing, advertising, attribution, or retargeting technologies if present.
- How users can manage preferences through browser settings, consent tools, account settings, or unsubscribe links.

## 6. How We Share Information

List categories of recipients rather than over-specific legal claims when exact vendors are unknown.

Common sharing categories:

- Hosting, database, infrastructure, CDN, and storage providers.
- Analytics and product measurement providers.
- Payment processors and billing providers.
- Authentication and identity providers.
- Email, notification, customer support, and CRM providers.
- AI model or automation providers.
- Advertising and attribution partners, if used.
- Professional advisers, auditors, insurers, and legal service providers.
- Authorities, regulators, courts, or law enforcement when required by law.
- Business transfer parties during merger, acquisition, financing, reorganization, or asset sale.

Do not state that the company does not sell or share personal information unless the user confirms this and the code does not contradict it.

## 7. Data Retention

Explain retention by category when known.

Common structure:

- Account data: retained while the account is active and for a reasonable period afterward.
- User content: retained while needed to provide the service or until deleted by the user, subject to backups and legal obligations.
- Billing records: retained as required for tax, accounting, fraud prevention, and legal obligations.
- Logs and analytics: retained for security, debugging, analytics, and product improvement for [Retention Period].
- Backups: deleted or overwritten according to backup cycles.

Use placeholders for unknown retention periods and flag them for confirmation.

## 8. User Rights And Choices

Cover rights based on the product and target regions without overclaiming compliance.

Common rights and choices:

- Access personal information.
- Correct inaccurate information.
- Request deletion.
- Request export or portability.
- Object to or restrict certain processing.
- Withdraw consent where processing is based on consent.
- Opt out of marketing emails.
- Manage cookies or tracking preferences.
- Delete an account through settings or by contacting support.

Include the request channel:

```text
To make a privacy request, contact us at [Privacy Contact Email].
```

## 9. Deletion Requests

Include this as a separate section when the user specifically cares about deletion, account deletion exists, or the product stores user content.

Explain:

- How users request deletion.
- Whether deletion removes account data, user content, or both.
- What may be retained after deletion, such as billing records, security logs, fraud prevention records, legal records, backups, or anonymized/aggregated data.
- Expected response timeline if known. Use a placeholder if unknown.

## 10. Security

Use practical, non-overpromising language.

State that reasonable administrative, technical, and organizational safeguards are used. Mention specific controls only when supported by project docs or user facts.

Avoid guarantees such as perfect security, complete encryption, no data loss, or breach-proof operation.

## 11. International Transfers

Use when users, vendors, infrastructure, or operator locations may cross borders.

Explain that information may be processed in countries other than the user's location and that privacy laws may differ. Add specific transfer mechanisms only when provided or documented.

## 12. Children's Privacy

State whether the service is intended for children.

If unknown, use conservative language that the service is not directed to children under [Age] and users should not provide information if they are under that age. Flag the age threshold for legal review.

## 13. Region-Specific Notices

Use only when target regions require additional language or the user asks for it.

Potential notices:

- EU/UK privacy rights and legal bases.
- California privacy rights and sale/share disclosures.
- Brazil LGPD rights.
- Canada privacy rights.

Do not claim compliance with a regional privacy law unless the user or docs support it. Use placeholders for missing legal bases, controller details, representative details, or opt-out mechanisms.

## 14. Changes To This Policy

Explain how updates are made, where the effective date appears, and how users may be notified of material changes.

## 15. Contact Us

Include company contact details:

```text
[Company Legal Name]
[Mailing Address, if required]
[Privacy Contact Email]
```

## Assumptions And Legal Review Notes

End with a short internal-facing section if facts are missing or if the user asked for a draft rather than publication-ready copy.

Include:

- Missing company/legal entity details.
- Missing privacy contact or mailing address.
- Missing retention periods.
- Missing vendor list or data processing terms.
- Unsupported compliance claims that were intentionally excluded.
- Clauses that require lawyer review before publication.

If creating a public-facing page, consider whether this section should stay in the Markdown source only or be removed from the rendered public copy.
