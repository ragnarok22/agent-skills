# BRANDING.md Template

Use this template as the expected structure and quality level for generated `BRANDING.md` files. Adapt every section to the current product. Do not keep placeholder text in the final file.

## Structure

# [Product Name] Branding Guide

## Brand Idea

Define the core emotional idea in one sentence or short paragraph.

Use a quote block when helpful:

> [Product Name] should feel like [specific emotional/visual concept].

State what the brand should not feel like.

List the core concept ingredients:

- [Symbol or metaphor] = [meaning]
- [Product domain cue] = [meaning]
- [Maker or technical cue, if relevant] = [meaning]

## Quick Brand Summary

Summarize the product, emotional promise, and main visual identity in 2-3 short paragraphs.

Name the 3-4 qualities the brand must balance. Explain that design, copy, and UI decisions should support those qualities.

## Brand Overview

Explain what the product is, where the name comes from if known, and why the visual identity fits the product.

Describe the brand as a product experience, not a generic company statement.

## Brand Essence

Provide a concise essence line.

Example format:

**[Short phrase. Short phrase.]**

Then explain what that phrase means in product and UI terms.

## Mission

State what the app helps users do. Keep it grounded and product-specific.

## Vision

State the longer-term change the product wants to make for users. Avoid inflated claims.

## Positioning

Define who the product is for, what it helps them accomplish, and what it is meaningfully different from.

Use this pattern when useful:

[Product Name] is a [category] for [audience] who want [outcome] without [pain or unwanted alternative].

## What The App Should Transmit

Create 3-5 named emotional or experiential pillars.

For each pillar, include:

### 1. [Pillar Name]

Users should feel:

> [Plain-language user feeling]

Not:

> [Opposite feeling to avoid]

Then explain design consequences: color, spacing, shapes, copy tone, density, hierarchy, or motion.

## Target Audience

Describe primary users, their practical context, and common emotions.

Include bullets for likely user groups and user thoughts.

Avoid inventing unsupported demographic claims.

## Brand Personality

List the personality traits the product should express.

Also list what to avoid.

Example:

- Calm
- Helpful
- Precise
- Warm

Avoid:

- Aggressive
- Overly playful
- Corporate

## Brand Name Presentation

Define how the name should appear in product UI, splash screens, landing pages, app stores, and icons.

Include casing, punctuation, parent brand usage, and whether text belongs in the app icon.

## Brand Architecture

Include this section only when there is a parent brand, maker brand, product family, or sub-brand relationship.

Explain which name users should remember and how supporting names should appear.

## Tagline Options

Provide 4-6 tagline options.

Then recommend one and explain why it fits the brand.

Avoid hype, exaggerated outcomes, and generic slogans.

## Brand Promise

State the practical and emotional promise the product can realistically keep.

## Functional Colors

Define colors for states relevant to the product.

Examples:

```text
Success / Positive: #[HEX]
Warning / Attention: #[HEX]
Error / Destructive: #[HEX]
Info: #[HEX]
Neutral: #[HEX]
Primary Action: #[HEX]
```

For finance products, consider income, expense, savings, transfer, and overbudget. For health products, consider progress, alert, recovery, and risk. Adapt to the product domain.

## Shapes

Include main shapes and what they communicate:

- Rounded rectangles = [meaning]

## Logo Direction

Describe the logo concept, not a final drawing.

Include:

- Main symbol direction.
- App icon direction.
- Whether to use text.
- Preferred background and foreground colors.
- Level of detail.
- Personality.
- What to avoid.

## UI Visual Direction

Translate the brand into product UI.

Include practical guidance for:

### Cards

- Radius.
- Borders or shadows.
- Density.
- Number treatment.
- Empty or content-heavy states.

### Charts Or Data Displays

- Recommended chart types.

- Labeling.
- What to avoid.

### Buttons

- Secondary button style.
- Destructive button style.

### Navigation

## Iconography

Define icon style:

- Line or filled.
- Stroke width.
- Corner radius.
- Level of detail.
- Good icon concepts.
- Forbidden icon directions.

Keep icon concepts connected to product meaning, not decoration.

## Illustration Style

Include:

- Style.
- Color usage.
- Human/mascot usage.
- Detail level.
- Empty states.
- Onboarding.
- What to avoid.

Do not create a mascot-heavy brand unless the product context supports it.

## Typography

Include:

- Optional warmer or more expressive heading font.
- Number styling if data-heavy.
- Weight rules.
- What typography should communicate.

## Emotional Experience


## Brand North Star

Provide one sentence that can guide every design, copy, and product decision.

## Decision Checklist

Before shipping a design, feature, copy change, or brand asset, check that it answers yes to most of these questions:

- Does it support the core emotional promise?
- Does it feel specific to this product and audience?
- Does it use the color and shape language consistently?
- Does it avoid the forbidden visual styles?
- Does it make the product easier to understand?
- Does it avoid exaggerated claims?
- Would the target user feel helped, respected, or motivated?

## Quality Rules

- Make every section specific to the product.
- Explain design consequences for emotional adjectives.
- Use concrete colors, shape rules, and UI guidance.
- Prefer one strong visual metaphor over several weak ones.
- Avoid business claims not supported by project context.
- Do not make the brand childish unless the audience and product clearly support it.
- Do not write a generic startup, SaaS, crypto, luxury, or corporate brand guide unless that is explicitly the right direction.
